How India's monsoon rainfall extremes are shifting (1951–2025)

Using 75 years of IMD 0.25° gridded daily rainfall, this project maps the climatology and variability of June–September (JJAS) rainfall and tracks how heavy rain above the 95th percentile has changed in frequency, intensity, location and timing. It also estimates extreme-value (GEV) return levels and compares early and late periods.

Ongoing work · preliminary figuresIMD gridded rainfallExtreme-value statistics

Preliminary findings

  • The centre of mass of heavy (above-Q95) monsoon rain moved about 59 km south and 89 km west between 1951 and 2025. Both drifts are statistically significant (about 8 and 13 km per decade; p ≈ 0.02).
  • The mean latitude of the extreme-rain belt shows only a weak southward tendency (about 2 km per decade) that is not significant (p = 0.45).
  • Compared with 1951–1987, the years 1988–2025 had more extreme-rain days at most latitudes, especially south of about 25°N and in the far north; the northern plains (about 26–30°N) saw slightly fewer.
  • Monsoon rain has become less concentrated in time over northwest India (a falling Precipitation Concentration Index), while the seasonal timing of rainfall has changed little (mostly within ±2 days per decade).
  • Changes in the 50-year return level between the early and late periods are patchy, reaching about ±40%, with widespread increases over central and western India and decreases over parts of the northeast.

Monsoon rainfall change over Rajasthan and Gujarat (1980–2023)

A regional study of India's arid and semi-arid northwest. It compares June–September rainfall between 1980–2001 and 2002–2023, maps monthly trends and changes in moderate, heavy and very heavy rainfall, and relates rainfall to four ocean index regions over the Arabian Sea and the Bay of Bengal.

Ongoing work · preliminary figuresRegional climateOcean–rainfall links

Preliminary findings

  • Average June–September rainfall in 2002–2023 was higher than in 1980–2001 over almost the whole region, by up to about 2–3 mm/day over Saurashtra and Kutch. Small decreases are confined to a few pockets of northeastern Rajasthan and southern Gujarat.
  • Seasonal rainfall trends are positive and significant over large parts of both states. September shows the strongest increases, especially over Gujarat and southern Rajasthan, while June trends are weakly negative over parts of southern Gujarat.
  • Moderate, heavy and very heavy rainfall have all increased over most of the region, most strongly over Gujarat.
  • Rainfall is negatively correlated with the northern Arabian Sea (NARA) index across most of the region (down to about −0.6 over Gujarat). Correlations with SARA and SBAY are weaker and mostly negative; NBAY shows weak positive correlations over Kutch, Saurashtra and western Rajasthan.

Carbon monoxide over India from MOPITT (2000–2024)

This project revisits the regional analysis of Girach and Nair (2014) with 25 years of MOPITT Version 10 thermal-infrared retrievals. It examines the vertical sensitivity of the retrievals, the seasonal cycle of upper-tropospheric CO, trends before and after mid-2014 across twelve land and ocean regions, and the CO anomaly during the 2020 COVID-19 lockdown.

Ongoing work · preliminary figuresSatellite remote sensingAtmospheric composition

Preliminary findings

  • In the upper troposphere (200 hPa), MOPITT Version 10 confirms rising CO during 2000–2014 in all twelve regions, but at about 1–1.5% per year, roughly half the 2–3% per year reported by Girach and Nair (2014) with Version 6.
  • After mid-2014 the upper-tropospheric increase stopped: 2014–2024 trends are slightly negative and mostly not significant.
  • Near the surface (900 hPa) the pattern is reversed: CO fell before 2014 and then levelled off or rose, with a significant change of slope in all twelve regions. Total-column trends stay within about ±0.5% per year.
  • There was no lockdown dip: in April–May 2020, CO was above its 2015–2019 level in most regions, by up to about 9% in the total column (13% at 900 hPa) over central India and 11% over the northern Arabian Sea. Only the eastern Indo-Gangetic Plain showed a small decrease.
  • The retrievals carry about 1.4 degrees of freedom for signal, so the 900 hPa and 200 hPa levels are only partly independent; the analysis accounts for this when comparing levels.

Key terms

A short glossary

Plain-language definitions of the terms used in the figures and findings on this page.

Cloudburst
An extreme burst of rain over a small area. The India Meteorological Department uses about 100 mm or more in one hour over roughly 20–30 km².
JJAS
June–September: the Indian summer monsoon season.
IMD rainfall categories
Daily rainfall of 15.6–64.4 mm is moderate, 64.5–115.5 mm heavy, 115.6–204.4 mm very heavy, and 204.5 mm or more extremely heavy.
Q95 (95th percentile)
The daily rainfall amount that is exceeded on only 5% of days at a location. Days above it are counted as extreme.
Coefficient of variation (CV)
Standard deviation divided by the mean: how much rainfall varies from year to year relative to its average.
Hovmöller diagram
A plot with time on one axis and latitude (or longitude) on the other, used to see how a pattern moves over the years.
Precipitation Concentration Index (PCI)
A measure of how evenly rain is spread across the season. Higher values mean the rain arrives in fewer, more concentrated spells.
GEV distribution
The Generalized Extreme Value distribution: a statistical model for the largest value in each block of time, such as each year's wettest day.
Return level
The rainfall amount expected to be exceeded on average once every N years. A 50-year return level has a 2% chance of being exceeded in any year.
Extremal coefficient θ(h)
How often extremes at two places a distance h apart happen together: θ = 1 means always together, θ = 2 means independent.
F-madogram and Brown–Resnick model
A way to estimate θ(h) from data, and a spatial model for extremes fitted to it. The fitted dependence range is the distance over which extremes stay strongly linked.
Mann–Kendall test and Sen's slope
A test for a steady upward or downward trend, and a robust estimate of its size (the median of all pairwise slopes).
Pettitt test
A test that finds the most likely single year when a time series changed its level.
WRF
The Weather Research and Forecasting model: a numerical weather prediction model that simulates the atmosphere on a grid.
3DVAR data assimilation
A method that blends observations with a model's first guess to give the best estimate of the starting state for a forecast.
Reanalysis (ERA5, IMDAA)
A consistent reconstruction of past weather made by combining a model with historical observations. ERA5 is global (ECMWF); IMDAA is India's regional reanalysis.
GPM and MSWEP
Gridded rainfall estimates from satellites (GPM) and from a merger of gauges, satellites and reanalysis (MSWEP).
Moisture transport (VIMT)
Vertically integrated moisture transport: the water vapour carried by the winds, summed through the depth of the atmosphere.
NARA, SARA, NBAY, SBAY
Ocean index boxes over the northern and southern Arabian Sea and the northern and southern Bay of Bengal (about 5–25°N, 55–100°E, split at 15°N and 77.5°E).
MOPITT
Measurements Of Pollution In The Troposphere: an instrument on NASA's Terra satellite that has measured carbon monoxide (CO) since 2000.
Averaging kernel and DFS
The averaging kernel shows which altitudes a satellite retrieval is actually sensitive to; the degrees of freedom for signal (DFS) count how many independent pieces of vertical information it contains.
IGP
The Indo-Gangetic Plain: the densely populated plains of northern India along the Indus and Ganga rivers.
