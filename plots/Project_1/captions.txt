Fig_1 :  Study domain and regions. (a) Mean total column CO from MOPITT Version 10 TIR Level 3 monthly data (daytime, grid-months with DFS > 1), March 2000–December 2024, with the twelve study regions of Girach and Nair (2014) outlined along the 1° × 1° cell edges (abbreviations as in Table 1). (b) Surface altitude of the MOPITT digital elevation model. (c) Density of Terra MODIS active-fire detections (presumed vegetation fires, confidence ≥ 30 %; 2001–2024). (d) Mean anthropogenic CO emission flux of CAMS-GLOB-ANT v6.2 (2000–2024). Panels (c) and (d) have logarithmic colour scales; white cells have no data or zero values. Map lines delineate study areas and do not necessarily depict accepted national boundaries; the Indian boundary is drawn from the shapefile used throughout this study.


Fig_2 : Mean averaging-kernel rows of the 900 hPa (vermilion circles, solid) and 200 hPa (blue squares, dashed) retrieval levels for the twelve regions (MOPITT Version 10 TIR Level 3 monthly data (daytime, grid-months with DFS > 1), March 2000–December 2024). A row gives the sensitivity of the retrieved log₁₀(VMR) at that level to the true log₁₀(VMR) at the ten levels of the retrieval grid (Sfc = surface). Symbols are means over the months of the region-mean kernels and error bars ± 1 standard deviation over the months; dotted lines are the means of June–September. The number in each panel is the mean DFS. All panels share the same axes.


Fig_3: Upper-tropospheric CO and retrieval diagnostics for four representative regions (columns: W-IGP, E-IGP, Central-I and N-BoB). (a–d) Monthly climatology of CO at the 200 hPa retrieval level: daytime retrievals with DFS > 1 for March 2000–December 2024 (thick blue; shading ± 1 interannual standard deviation), their a priori (grey dashed), night-time retrievals (dark dotted) and daytime retrievals for March 2000–May 2014 (light blue) and June 2014–December 2024 (orange). (e–h) Averaging-kernel row sums of the 200 hPa (blue) and 900 hPa (vermilion) levels and DFS (black). (i–l) Mean number of daytime Level 2 retrievals per 1° cell and month. MOPITT Version 10 TIR Level 3; regional means; grey shading marks June–September; y axes are identical along each row.


Fig_4 : Deseasonalised monthly CO anomalies at the 900 hPa (vermilion) and 200 hPa (blue) retrieval levels for the twelve regions (MOPITT Version 10 TIR Level 3 monthly data (daytime, grid-months with DFS > 1), March 2000–December 2024; anomalies from the monthly climatology of the whole period). Thin lines: monthly values; thick lines: centred 12-month running means; straight lines: continuous piecewise-linear fits with the break at May/June 2014 (dashed vertical line), the end of the record of Girach and Nair (2014). Numbers are the slopes before → after the break (ppb yr⁻¹); an asterisk marks a significant change of slope. Grey bands mark the instrument outages and the light-yellow band the first instrument phase (March 2000–May 2001). The fits contain no term for the offset of the first phase, which therefore steepens both segments (Section 3.7.3). The series end in July 2024.

Fig_5 :  Linear trends (% yr⁻¹) of CO at (a) the 900 hPa and (b) the 200 hPa retrieval levels and (c) in the total column for the twelve regions: values of Girach and Nair (2014) for MOPITT Version 6, March 2000–May 2014 (hatched; open bars were not significant in their study), and MOPITT Version 10 (daytime, DFS > 1) for March 2000–May 2014 (light blue), June 2014–December 2024 (orange) and March 2000–December 2024 (black). Error bars are 95 % confidence intervals from ordinary least squares with AR(1)-adjusted effective sample size; filled circles mark trends significant (p < 0.05) in both that test and the modified Mann–Kendall test, open circles in one of them.

Fig_6: CO during the national COVID-19 lockdown (25 March–31 May 2020). (a–d) Anomalies (%) of the total column and of CO at the 900 hPa retrieval level in April and May 2020 relative to the same months of 2015–2019 (MOPITT Version 10 TIR Level 3 monthly data (daytime, grid-months with DFS > 1)). (e, f) April–May 2020 anomalies of the regional means relative to three baseline periods and to the linear trend line of 2010–2024 fitted without 2020; dots mark anomalies exceeding two standard deviations. All maps share one colour scale; thin outlines mark the study regions.
